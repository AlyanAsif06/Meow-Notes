The Ethical System of Islam
1. Concept of Good & Bad Ethics in Islam
Islam defines ethics based on divine guidance rather than subjective human opinions. Good ethics are those endorsed by Allah and His Messenger (PBUH), while bad ethics are those prohibited by them.

Good ethics include: honesty, justice, compassion, patience, humility, and sincerity.

Bad ethics include: lying, oppression, arrogance, backbiting, envy, and deceit.

Example:
In the Qur'an (16:90), Allah commands justice, kindness, and generosity, and forbids indecency, oppression, and rebellion.

2. Self-Purification (Tazkiyah al-Nafs)
Self-purification is the process of cleansing one's soul from spiritual diseases such as arrogance, jealousy, greed, and hypocrisy, and replacing them with virtues.

It is an ongoing inner struggle (Jihad al-Nafs).

Example:
The Prophet (PBUH) said: "The strongest among you is the one who controls himself when he is angry." (Bukhari & Muslim) – reflecting the inner self-purification of anger management.

3. Sidq (Truthfulness)
Truthfulness is a core ethical principle in Islam, covering speech, action, and intention.

The Prophet (PBUH) was known as Al-Sadiq (The Truthful).

Lies are strictly prohibited even in joking.

Example:
The Prophet (PBUH) said: "Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise... while lying leads to sin, and sin leads to Hellfire." (Bukhari & Muslim)

4. Sakhawat (Generosity)
Islam encourages Muslims to be generous, not only in wealth but also in time, knowledge, and good conduct.

Generosity is considered a sign of faith.

Example:
The Prophet (PBUH) was described as "more generous than the blowing wind" (Bukhari), especially during Ramadan.

5. Tawakkul (Trust in Allah)
Tawakkul is having complete trust and reliance on Allah while taking appropriate actions.

It is not passiveness but a balance of action and faith.

Example:
The Prophet (PBUH) said: "Tie your camel and then put your trust in Allah." (Tirmidhi) – showing that effort and reliance go hand in hand.

6. Sabr (Patience)
Patience is a highly valued virtue in Islam, needed in facing hardships, performing duties, and avoiding sins.

Allah promises great rewards for those who observe patience.

Example:
The Qur'an says: "Indeed, Allah is with those who are patient." (Surah Al-Baqarah: 153)

7. Taqwa (Piety, God-consciousness)
Taqwa is the consciousness of Allah in all actions, leading a person to obey Him and avoid disobedience.

It is the most honored quality in Islam, surpassing lineage, wealth, and status.

Example:s
The Prophet (PBUH) said: "The most honorable of you in the sight of Allah is the most God-fearing (Muttaqi)." (Qur'an 49:13)

Conclusion
The Islamic ethical system is holistic, guiding both private and public life. It promotes personal development, social harmony, and a strong relationship with Allah, ensuring that Muslims become not only law-abiding individuals but also morally upright and spiritually conscious beings.
